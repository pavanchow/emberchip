Emberchip

A deterministic RTOS, running in your browser. Watch a preemptive fixed-priority scheduler, a blinking LED, and priority inheritance defeat priority inversion.

Honest framing: a real embedded OS runs as firmware on a microcontroller. This is a teaching-accurate simulator of one, reimplemented in plain JavaScript to mirror the Rust engine. It models the mechanisms, it is not firmware for a real chip.
Source and the full engine: github.com/pavanchow/emberchip

How to use this playground

    
Every control, and what to look for.

    
    
      
  • Scenario: choose the task set. Blinky is a lively schedulable set with a blinking LED and UART chatter. Priority inversion is the classic three-task trap for watching priority inheritance.
    • 
      
  • Priority inheritance: turns the mechanism on or off. It matters most in the inversion scenario. Toggling it resets the run.
    • 
      
  • Step S: advance exactly one tick. Best for watching the mechanism closely.
    • 
      
  • Run / Pause Space: play continuously at the chosen speed.
    • 
      
  • Reset R: rebuild the current scenario from tick zero.
    • 
      
  • Speed: how many ticks per second when running.
    • 
    


    
Guided tour

    
    
      
  1. Leave the scenario on Priority inversion with inheritance on. Press Run.
    2. 
      
  2. Watch low take the mutex (green L), then high arrive and block (amber cell, and a red BLOCK line in the log).
    3. 
      
  3. See the purple INHERIT line: low is boosted to high's priority, so medium cannot steal the CPU. Low finishes its short critical section and hands the mutex straight to high.
    4. 
      
  4. Note the high blocked readout. It is small, bounded by the critical section.
    5. 
      
  5. Now turn inheritance off and press Run again. This time medium preempts low while high waits, and the high blocked readout balloons. That is unbounded priority inversion.
    6. 
      
  6. Switch to Blinky and Run. Watch the LED toggle from the GPIO task, UART text stream, and higher-priority tasks preempt the workers, all with zero deadline misses.
